🌍 Hello from Three Countries!

Whether you are reading this in Hesse (Germany), Gloucestershire (UK), or right here in Kavutiri, Kenya — you are all connected to the same amazing farm where real coffee cherries grow!

This farm sits in the highlands of Kenya at about 1,600 metres above sea level. That's higher than most mountains in the UK! The farmers here watch the weather every single day to know when to water, when to harvest, and when to protect their plants. Let's follow the farm through its whole year! 🗓️

📖 Word Explorer — What do these words mean?

☔ Precipitation (mm) A fancy science word for rain! We measure it in millimetres — like how tall the rainwater would be if none soaked into the ground.
🌡 Temperature (°C) How hot or cold the air is, measured in degrees Celsius. Kavutiri stays between 17°C and 24°C all year — never freezing, never scorching!
💧 ET0 — Evapotranspiration How thirsty the air is! ET0 tells us how much water plants and soil lose into the air each day. High ET0 = plants need more water.
🪣 Water Balance Like a bank account for water! Rain goes IN. Thirsty air and plants take water OUT. If the balance is negative, it's time to irrigate.
🚿 Irrigation Watering plants on purpose when there isn't enough rain. Farmers at Kavutiri irrigate most in January and February.
🍒 Coffee Cherry The fruit of the coffee tree! Each red cherry holds 2 seeds inside — those seeds, once processed and roasted, become the coffee in your cup.
🌍 Long Rains vs Short Rains Kenya has TWO rainy seasons! Long Rains = March–May (bigger). Short Rains = October–December (shorter). Very different from Germany or the UK!
🌱 Nursery Not a baby nursery — a plant nursery! A special sheltered place where tiny coffee seedlings grow strong before being planted out.
